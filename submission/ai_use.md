# Use of Super Intelligence (SI)

**The idea is ours.** The team came up with FLARE, a tool that ranks and compares NASA's combustion
investigations against a fire scenario someone describes, without an AI tool. AI sped up the
building; it did not choose the problem, the approach or the data.

## How we used AI to build FLARE

All six of us built FLARE with **Claude Code**, Anthropic's coding agent, running **Claude Opus
5.5** and, for part of the work, **Claude Fable 5.2**. Each person directed their own agent on their
own task, in a shared GitHub repository. The agents wrote most of the code and documentation under
our direction; we chose each task's scope, made the design choices, read the results, ran the
tests and decided what to merge.

What the agents did, by area:

- **Data:** wrote the scripts that read NASA PSI's records, file lists and experimental tables, put
  the 22 tables into one comparable shape (every original value kept), and profiled them for
  suspect values. The data itself is NASA's: no AI generated or changed any of it.
- **Search and ranking:** wrote the document search index and the ranking that scores each
  investigation's tests against a scenario and explains every score.
- **Model:** wrote the training script for FLARE's one model (on NASA's FLEX tests) against a
  simple baseline, held-out testing and SHAP explanations.
- **Website:** built the pages from design directions the team chose among (using the
  *impeccable* design skill), and fixed the problems our QA found.
- **Teamwork and deployment:** wrote the rules and the small task server that keep six agents from
  editing the same files, and the Vercel deployment settings.

The agents also used public "skills", instruction packs for Claude Code, for design, data analysis,
planning, debugging and checking work before calling it done. They are listed in References.

## AI inside FLARE

FLARE's research assistant uses **Claude Opus 5.5** through the Anthropic API. It answers only from
NASA sources that FLARE retrieves (investigation records and passages from PSI documents) and ties
each sentence to the source it uses. Its answers are labelled on screen as "AI-generated" with the
model's name. Without an API key, the assistant still works: it shows the matching NASA passages
and facts, with links, and no generated text.

Summaries of each investigation are written by the same assistant ahead of time, stored with the
model's name and a "reviewed" mark that a team member sets only after checking the summary against
its sources.

## How AI-made content is marked

- **Code and data:** every commit an agent helped write names the model in a `Co-Authored-By`
  line. At the time of writing, 28 of the 32 commits on our main branch carry one. No NASA data
  was generated by AI; everything FLARE derives from it is produced by scripts in the repository.
- **Text in the product:** the assistant's answers and stored summaries are labelled
  AI-generated, with the model's name.
- **Images:** our team logo is our own work, not made with AI. The illustrations in our pitch
  opening, the two flame drawings on FLARE's Methodology and Scenario pages and the astronaut on its
  Home page were made with AI (Claude Code) and each carries a visible "Illustration made with AI" watermark. No AI-made
  content uses or alters NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers.
- **No other AI tools** were used outside the repository.

Every member keeps a log of what their agent did and who checked it, in `docs/ai-use/` in our
repository.
