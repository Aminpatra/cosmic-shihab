# Judging criteria

Source: NASA Space Apps Challenge, *Judging & Awards Guide* (spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide,
dated 13 November 2026). Check every feature, slide and claim against this file before calling it done.

## Hard requirements (miss one and we can't go to Global Judging)

- [ ] **Deadline:** submitted on the Project tab (Edit → Submit) before **Sunday 15 November 2026, 11:59 p.m.**
      local time of our Local Event.
- [ ] Responds to one official challenge: **Flame in Freefall**.
- [ ] Every member is registered for the same Local Event (not waitlisted) and shows on the team's Members tab.
      At most 6 members. Members can't be added after submission closes.
- [ ] Every required field on the Project Submission page is filled in.
- [ ] **Demo:** either a slide deck of **at most 7 slides** (title and intro slides count) **or** a video of
      **at most 30 seconds**.
- [ ] Everything is in **English**: the submission page and all linked material. A video may be spoken in
      another language only if it has English subtitles.
- [ ] **References field lists every external resource** we used: code, libraries, datasets, text, images, fonts,
      even open-source or free ones. No copyrighted material (music, images, text) we lack permission to use.
- [ ] Both checkboxes ticked: the Submission Guide / Terms / Privacy agreement, and the original-work declaration.
- [ ] No prohibited bilateral activity with China or a Chinese-owned entity (NASA reviews this).

## From the Project Submission Guide

Source: NASA Space Apps Challenge, *Project Submission Guide* (dated 13 November 2026).

**The form.** Submission opened Saturday 14 November at 9:00 a.m. local. We can save and resubmit
as often as we like; only the last saved submission is judged. The fields are:

- [ ] Project Name
- [ ] High-Level Project Summary: what we built, how it addresses the challenge, why it matters
- [ ] Link to Project Demo (the 7 slides or the 30-second video)
- [ ] Link to Final Project (the deployed site or the repository)
- [ ] Detailed Project Description: what it does, how it works, benefits, goals, tools and languages
- [ ] Use of Super Intelligence (SI): written from `docs/ai-use/`
- [ ] NASA Data: specific details of the PSI data and resources used (at least one is needed for a
      Global Award)
- [ ] Space Agency Partner Data and Other References

**Links.**

- [ ] Every link opens with no login, permission or registration. Test each one in a private
      browser window before submitting. A project the judges can't open isn't judged.
- [ ] The page takes images only (JPG, PNG, GIF, up to 10 MB). Videos and documents are linked.
- [ ] Recommended for a new website: request a FortiGuard Web Filter Classification for the
      deployed URL so judges' networks don't block it (company name "N/A").

**SI use.** SI tools are allowed to speed up the work, not to come up with the idea.

- [ ] SI-made images and videos carry a visible watermark.
- [ ] SI-made audio is acknowledged in descriptive text and metadata.
- [ ] SI-made code and data are acknowledged in descriptive text and metadata.
- [ ] No SI-made content uses or alters NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers.

Judges don't see the SI answer itself, but they see these marks in the project and judge
originality by them.

**Demo content.**

- [ ] A slide deck shows the team name, the members' names and the challenge chosen.
- [ ] Nothing shows the image, video, audio or likeness of anyone under 18.

## Scoring: five criteria, each scored 1 to 5

| Criterion | What judges look for | What a 5 means | What it means for FLARE |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Impact** | How big the problem is and how many people the solution helps or inspires. | Addresses a significant problem; wide reach; benefits a large audience or could change the world. | Show who it helps (researchers, mission planners, fire-safety engineers) and the real decision it speeds up, e.g. picking the right prior PSI investigation for a new scenario. Tie microgravity flame research to spacecraft fire safety and to fire science on Earth. |
| **Creativity** | Originality: a new concept, or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas. | A groundbreaking approach never tried before that challenges conventional methods. | Our angle is scenario-driven *ranking and comparison* of investigations, not just a searchable list. Make that difference obvious in the first seconds of the demo. |
| **Validity** | Scientifically grounded; technically, functionally or logically feasible; usable and user-friendly. | Scientifically sound, fully developed, ready for real use, highly usable. | A working, deployed demo, not mock-ups. ML judged against a simple baseline and reported on held-out data only. Explain each ranking (e.g. SHAP). Check every screen at 375 px and desktop. Never show a number a run didn't produce. |
| **Relevance** | Fully addresses the challenge; NASA data is a core component, not decoration. | NASA data is integral and drives the insight; data use is well executed. | PSI data must power the core feature (the ranking and comparison). Name the datasets, show where each number comes from, and cover what the Flame in Freefall brief asks for. |
| **Presentation** | Clear, engaging, well-structured story of challenge → solution → why it matters. Judges score the demo *and* the full project. | Polished, compelling storytelling; the audience fully grasps the significance. | Give the 7-slide or 30-second demo real time and design effort, not a last-hour job. One story: the problem, FLARE in use on a real scenario, the result, why it matters. |

The guide stresses that teams often under-invest in **Presentation** and should give it equal weight to the other four.

## Global Awards we fit best

Global Finalists are reviewed by an executive committee from NASA and partner agencies, who nominate teams for
one of ten awards. The ones closest to FLARE:

- **Best Use of Data**: makes space data accessible or uses it in a unique application. *(Our strongest fit.)*
- **Best Use of Science**: the best and most valid use of the scientific method. *(Baselines, held-out metrics,
  honest limits.)*
- **Best Storytelling**: communicates the potential of open data creatively through storytelling.
- **Best Use of Technology**: the most innovative use of technology.

The other six are Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Global Connection, Art & Technology
and Local Impact.

## Quick check before calling anything done

1. Does it make the impact obvious to someone outside combustion science? (Impact)
2. Is it something other tools don't already do, and does the demo show that? (Creativity)
3. Does it work, on real data, at phone and desktop width, with numbers we can back up? (Validity)
4. Is NASA PSI data doing the work, and is that visible? (Relevance)
5. Can it be explained in 7 slides or 30 seconds? (Presentation)
6. Is every external resource it uses written down for the References field? (Eligibility)
